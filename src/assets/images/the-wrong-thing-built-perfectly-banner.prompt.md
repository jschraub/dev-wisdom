## Why this subject

The article's mechanism is convergence. Two problems walked into the design
session believing they were unrelated, a TLS entry point and a browser socket
ceiling, and the interrogation refused to treat them as two projects. What came
out was one design that resolved both, because the component that terminates TLS
is the same component that can end the connection fan-out.

So the left half holds **two separate tangles, not one**. Each is dense and
self-contained, and critically **they do not touch** — that separation is the
subject. Banner #1's left half was a disordered swarm and #2's was an
undifferentiated field; this one is two orderly-but-isolated knots, which is a
third distinct failure state and must not read as either of the earlier two.

Moving right, strands from **both** tangles draw toward and pass through a single
narrow aperture, then emerge as one ordered fan of evenly spaced parallel lines.
Nothing is discarded. Everything from both knots comes through. It is the same
material, routed through one point instead of two hundred, which is exactly what
multiplexing at the gateway did and exactly what the article's inline SVG shows.

**The gold is the aperture itself**, the single point everything passes through,
and it is the only warm note in the frame. Per the gold rule it marks the one
thing proven: not the tangles, not the fan, but the agreed design that both
parties could describe, which is the artifact this gate produces.
